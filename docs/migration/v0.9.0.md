# Migrating to v0.9.0

## Signal control: pedestrian detectors

Pedestrian detectors are no longer vehicle detectors with a pedestrian
type. They have their own table, `pedestrian-detectors/pedestrian-detector`,
beside `detectors/detector`.

- **Key.** `pedestrian-detector-id`, 1..255. Pedestrian detectors are
  numbered independently of vehicle detectors, so pedestrian detector 1 and
  vehicle detector 1 are different devices.
- **Config.** `name`, `enabled`, `type` (`pedestrian-pushbutton` or
  `pedestrian-passive`), a mandatory `call-phase` (the single phase the
  detector places its call on; it must resolve to an existing
  `phases/phase`), and `diagnostic-thresholds` with `no-activity-min`,
  `max-presence-min` and `erratic-counts-per-minute`.
- **State.** Observed activity only: `active`, `last-activation`,
  `actuation-count` and `fault`, the same as a vehicle detector. There is no
  config mirror; read `name`, `type`, `call-phase` and the thresholds from
  `config`.
- **Vehicle detectors.** The `detector-type` identities
  `detector-ped-pushbutton` and `detector-ped-passive` are removed. A vehicle
  detector no longer carries pedestrian types, and the pedestrian table has
  none of the vehicle-only leaves (such as `delay-s`, `extend-s`,
  `extend-phases` and the measurement).
- **Diagnostics.** Both tables take their thresholds from one shared
  `detector-diagnostics` grouping, in a container named
  `diagnostic-thresholds`. The vehicle container `detectors/detector/config/
  diagnostics` is renamed to `diagnostic-thresholds` to match.
- **ARC-IT flow.** `pedestrian-detectors` is annotated with the same
  ARC-IT Vehicle/Pedestrian detection flow as `detectors`.

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| `detectors/detector` with `type` = `detector-ped-pushbutton` | `pedestrian-detectors/pedestrian-detector` with `type` = `pedestrian-pushbutton` |
| `detectors/detector` with `type` = `detector-ped-passive` | `pedestrian-detectors/pedestrian-detector` with `type` = `pedestrian-passive` |
| `detectors/detector/config/diagnostics` | `detectors/detector/config/diagnostic-thresholds` |
| `assigned-phases` (list) | `call-phase` (single, mandatory) |

Move each pedestrian entry out of `detectors/detector`, give it a
`pedestrian-detector-id`, and set its `call-phase`.

Protobuf consumers: the vehicle `diagnostics` field (10) of
`openits.signal_control.v1.DetectorsDetectorConfig` is replaced by
`diagnostic_thresholds` (14), and the old number and name are reserved. Its
message moves from `openits.signal_control.v1.Diagnostics` to
`DetectorsDetectorConfigDiagnosticThresholds`, whose fields are
`erratic_counts_per_minute` (1), `max_presence_min` (2) and
`no_activity_min` (3). Stored or in-flight bytes carry the old field as
unknown, so re-encode or translate them.

## Reversible lane: lane-use signals

The lane-use signal (LCS) model now follows MUTCD 11th Edition Chapter 4T.

### Indications

`lcs-indication` is the type of `lcs-direction-a` and `lcs-direction-b` on
`segments/segment/lane/config` and `state`, and of the indications in
`lcs-conflict-detected` and `lcs-indication-changed`. Existing values keep
their numbers.

| Before | After | Value |
|---|---|---|
| `green-arrow` | `downward-green-arrow` (MUTCD 11th Edition Section 4T.02 A) | 5, unchanged |
| none | `white-two-way-left-turn-arrow` (Section 4T.02 D) | 6, new |
| none | `white-one-way-left-turn-arrow` (Section 4T.02 E) | 7, new |
| `flashing-yellow-x` | `flashing-yellow-x`, now legacy | 4, unchanged |

`flashing-yellow-x` is a pre-2009 left-turn-use indication, not a current
MUTCD indication. A device may still report it, so it stays in the type, but
a commanded `lcs-direction-a` or `lcs-direction-b` of `flashing-yellow-x` is
refused. Rename `green-arrow` to `downward-green-arrow` in stored config and
in instance data. JSON and YANG consumers see the new name. In protobuf the
enum member `LCS_INDICATION_GREEN_ARROW` (5) is now
`LCS_INDICATION_DOWNWARD_GREEN_ARROW` (5), and the new members are
`LCS_INDICATION_WHITE_TWO_WAY_LEFT_TURN_ARROW` (6) and
`LCS_INDICATION_WHITE_ONE_WAY_LEFT_TURN_ARROW` (7). The number is unchanged,
so bytes on the wire stay valid; generated code must use the new name.

### Commanded-config rules

`lane/config` replaces its old green-arrow and flashing-yellow-x rules with
these. Each is a `must` on the commanded pair, and a config that breaks one
is refused:

| Rule | Refused when |
|---|---|
| Green needs the opposing red X (Section 4T.04) | one direction is `downward-green-arrow` and the other is not `red-x` |
| Section 4T.04 B | `yellow-x` is commanded to both directions |
| Section 4T.04 C | `white-one-way-left-turn-arrow` is commanded to both directions |
| Section 4T.04 F | `white-two-way-left-turn-arrow` is commanded to one direction and `white-one-way-left-turn-arrow` to the other |
| Section 4T.04 G | `white-one-way-left-turn-arrow` is commanded to one direction and `yellow-x` to the other |
| Legacy | either direction is `flashing-yellow-x` |

`lane/state` carries no `must`. A device that reads back a prohibited pair
(for example a downward green arrow to both directions) still reports it as
data, together with a fault and `lcs-conflict-detected`.

### Events

- **`lcs-indication-changed` (new).** One signal head changed indication.
  It carries `segment-id`, `lane-id`, `face` (a `carriageway-direction`
  identity), an optional `previous-indication` and a mandatory `indication`,
  plus the shared event header and `wire-source`. Its `kind` is
  `rl-lcs-indication-changed`. A consumer replays these in `sequence` order
  per head to check a changeover against Section 4T.04: a downward green arrow
  ends in a `yellow-x` or `red-x`, and a `yellow-x` ends in a `red-x`.
  The conformance kind `reversible-lane` has a check for this.
- **`lcs-conflict-detected` (widened).** It now means any read-back pair
  Section 4T.04 prohibits over one lane, a downward green arrow without a
  steady red X to the opposite direction, or any other conflict the
  vendor detects. Before, it covered only green arrows to both directions.
  The fields are unchanged; the indications use the renamed type.
- **`lane-state-changed`.** `new-state` is now `state` and `new-direction`
  is now `direction`. `previous-state` and `previous-direction` keep their
  names.
- **`kind` sub-bases.** `kind` on `lane-state-changed`, `lcs-conflict-detected`,
  `transition-timeout` and `lcs-indication-changed` is now constrained to a
  sub-base per notification: `rl-lane-state-change-event-kind`,
  `rl-lcs-conflict-event-kind`, `rl-transition-timeout-event-kind` and
  `rl-lcs-indication-change-event-kind`. The concrete kind identities
  (`rl-lane-state-changed`, `rl-lcs-conflict-detected`, `rl-transition-timeout`)
  keep their names and their wire values.

### Control source

`control/state/last-command-source`, a free-form string, is split in two:

| Leaf | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `control-source` | identityref, base `openits-types:control-source` | the authority class of the most recent command (central, local backup, manual, schedule, ...) |
| `last-command-by` | string | the operator or system that issued it, as the device reports it |

Read the authority from `control-source` and the issuing actor from
`last-command-by`. `last-command-at` is unchanged.

### Federal profile and state profiles

`openits-reversible-lane-mutcd`, under `yang/deviations/`, is the federal
profile. It replaces the type of `lcs-direction-a` and `lcs-direction-b` on
`lane/config` with the current Chapter 4T indications (everything except
`flashing-yellow-x`) and leaves `state` on the full set, so a device can still
report what it shows. The core already refuses `flashing-yellow-x`, so the
profile adds no rule today; it names the deployment as following the federal
set and is the template for a state profile.

A state whose manual is stricter publishes its own deviation module in the
same form. For a state that bans the white left-turn arrows, list only
`unknown`, `dark`, `red-x`, `yellow-x` and `downward-green-arrow` in the
replacement enumeration, keeping each member's value.

### Protobuf

All in package `openits.reversible_lane.v1`.

| Message | Change |
|---|---|
| `LcsIndication` | `LCS_INDICATION_GREEN_ARROW` (5) renamed `LCS_INDICATION_DOWNWARD_GREEN_ARROW` (5); `LCS_INDICATION_WHITE_TWO_WAY_LEFT_TURN_ARROW` (6) and `LCS_INDICATION_WHITE_ONE_WAY_LEFT_TURN_ARROW` (7) added |
| `ControlState` | `last_command_source` (15) removed, number and name reserved; `control_source` (2, string) and `last_command_by` (3, string) added |
| `LaneStateChanged` | `new_state` (3) and `new_direction` (4) removed, numbers and names reserved; `state` (12) and `direction` (13) added |
| `LcsIndicationChanged` | new message: `segment_id` (1), `lane_id` (2), `face` (3), `previous_indication` (4), `indication` (5), `observed_by` (6), `occurred_at` (7), `owner` (8), `sequence` (9), `sequence_epoch` (10), `source_device_id` (11), `kind` (99), `source` (100) |

The `LcsIndication` change breaks generated code that names the enum member,
not the wire. The `ControlState` and `LaneStateChanged` changes move fields,
so stored or in-flight bytes carry the old numbers as unknown fields:
re-encode or translate them.

## Detector events

Detector events name the detector by its number in its own table.

| Event | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| `detector-transition` | `channel` | `detector-id` (vehicle detector number, mandatory) |
| `pedestrian-event` | `detector-channel` | `pedestrian-detector-id` (pedestrian detector number, optional) |

Protobuf consumers: each renamed field moves to a NEW field number and the
old number and name are reserved. v0.9.0 decoders treat the old field in
stored or in-flight bytes as unknown, so re-encode or translate stored data.
The protobuf moves:

- `DetectorTransition`: `channel` (2) to `detector_id` (11).
- `PedestrianEvent`: `detector_channel` (3) to `pedestrian_detector_id` (9).

## Unit-suffixed names

In signal control (and the shared NEMA phase timing), every leaf that holds
a duration, interval, delay or percentage now ends in its unit: `-s` for seconds, `-min` for minutes, `-pct` for percent. Only the
leaf name changes; types, units and values are unchanged, and enum members
and identities that share a word with a leaf (for example the
`trailing-green` enum value) keep their names. The `tsp-event`
`adjustment-s` leaf also gained its missing `units "seconds"`.

Protobuf consumers: as in "Detector events", every renamed field moves to a
new field number and the old number and name are reserved, so stored or
in-flight bytes carry the old fields as unknown. Re-encode or translate
stored data. The regenerated `.proto` files under `api/proto/openits/` list
each reservation. The protobuf moves:

- `openits.types.v1.ScOnBatteryPolicy`: `transition_at_runtime_minutes` (3)
  to `transition_at_runtime_min` (4). Path:
  `cabinet-power/{config,state}/on-battery-policy`.
- `openits.ramp_metering.v1.PhaseTiming`: `max_green` (1), `min_green` (2),
  `passage` (3), `red_clear` (4) and `yellow_change` (5) move to
  `max_green_s` (6), `min_green_s` (7), `passage_s` (8), `red_clear_s` (9)
  and `yellow_change_s` (10).
- `DetectorReportDetector`: `occupancy` (4) to `occupancy_pct` (5).
- `TsamRecommendationApplied`: `magnitude_seconds` (4) to `magnitude_s` (12).
- `TspEvent`: `adjustment_seconds` (5) to `adjustment_s` (12).

(`phases/phase` timing). Ramp metering composes its vehicle subset,
`vehicle-phase-timing` (all but `walk` and `ped-clear`), for the metering
signal timing, so its leaves are renamed too. The signal-control MUTCD
deviation modules target the renamed paths.
